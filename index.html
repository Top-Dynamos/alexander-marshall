The situation

You already have a reputation. It is just scattered.

If you have been working for twenty or thirty years, your name already returns results. A directory listing somebody else wrote. A profile you last updated years ago. A company biography that stops being true the day you leave. An interview from 2019 that still sits near the top.

None of it is wrong exactly. It is just partial, out of date, and written by other people. The accomplishments that actually matter are usually the ones hardest to find.

That gap is the whole job.

The people

People who already have something to show.

This is not for someone starting out. It is for people who have accomplishments, income or a reputation already worth strengthening.

  • Doctors and dentists
  • Attorneys
  • Executives
  • Business owners
  • Entrepreneurs
  • Real estate
  • Financial professionals
  • Consultants
  • Professors
  • Retired senior professionals

Often the best fit is the person who says they do not need this. “I already have a website.” “I have been featured before.” “People already know who I am.” That is usually someone with plenty of credibility and very little control over where it shows up.

For business owners

If you run the company, the name people search is still yours.

  • It builds trust before the first conversation.
  • It brings in customers who found you, rather than the other way round.
  • It improves what shows up when someone checks you out.
  • It protects the reputation you have spent years building.
  • It puts you in front of your industry as someone worth listening to.

I run a company myself. This is not theory to me.

Background

Entrepreneur first.

I own Top Dynamos, the firm behind this work, and I have been building personal brands since 2015.

Before that I spent my career in numismatic coins, which is a business built almost entirely on provenance and reputation: what a thing is, who says so, and whether the record holds up. The same three questions turn out to decide how a professional is seen online.

I work from Long Island, New York, with clients across the country.

In coins, a piece is worth what its record can prove. Strip out the record and you are left with metal. Reputation works the same way.
